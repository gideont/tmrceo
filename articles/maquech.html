Meet the Maquech: this jewel-covered beetle is alive!

Yes, it’s alive! In a tradition from Mexico’s Yucatán Peninsula, a living Maquech beetle is decorated and worn as jewellery.

By tmrCEO curiosity desk · October 16, 2026· 2 min read

Imagine a brooch that starts walking. A maquech, also spelled makech, is a real beetle that has been used as living jewellery. Its decorations are attached to its body, rather than embedded inside it.

A beetle beneath the sparkle

The Natural History Museum in London identifies the maquech as Zopherus chilensis, a flightless beetle. Its undecorated body has brown, grey and black markings that help it blend into bark covered with lichens.

The museum connects the jewellery tradition to Mexico’s Yucatán Peninsula. Its collection includes a nineteenth-century example decorated and attached to a chain.

Why the tiny chain?

Because the “jewel” has legs. The University of Colorado Museum describes a specimen adorned with ribbon and an opalescent material, with a chain that could be pinned to clothing. The chain connects the living insect to its wearer.

A question worth asking

The Colorado museum asks visitors to think about the boundary between a pet and an accessory. Learning about a tradition can also make us curious about an animal’s needs. We can appreciate the story without decorating a beetle ourselves.
